The 5 Unwritten Rules of Surf Etiquette in Morocco

The lineup can be a busy place. Learn these simple rules to keep everyone safe and having a great time.

Morocco’s waves are world-class, and that means the lineup can get busy — especially at famous points like Anchor Point or Hash Point near Taghazout. The good news? A few simple rules keep everyone safe and smiling. Whether you’re a first-timer or a seasoned traveller, here are the five unwritten rules you need to know.

1. Don’t Drop In

If someone is already riding a wave, it’s theirs. Dropping in on another surfer is the fastest way to make enemies in the water. Always look toward the peak before you take off, and if you’re not sure — wait.

2. Don’t Snake the Lineup

Paddling around someone who is closest to the peak to steal their priority is called snaking, and it’s considered a huge no-no. Take your turn in the rotation, respect the pecking order, and result you’ll be thanked by fellow surfers.

3. Respect the Right of Way

When two surfers are paddling for the same wave, the surfer closest to the peak has the right of way. On a left, the surfer on the left has priority; on a right, the surfer on the right. Learn to read the line-up and give way when needed.

4. Don’t Paddle Through the Peak

Paddling directly through the breaking "peak" disrupts the wave for everyone. Instead, paddle wide around the outside or take the whitewater channel. Watch where riders are going before crossing their path.

5. Respect Locals and the Ocean

The local surfers know these breaks better than anyone — show them respect, greet them, and follow their lead. And finally, always look after the ocean: don’t leave trash on the beach, and reef-safe sunscreen is the way to go.

Our tip: when you paddle out at a new break, spend the first few minutes watching. You’ll learn more from the locals in ten minutes than from a year of books.
